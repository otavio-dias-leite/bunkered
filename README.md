# Bunkered

A survival bunker simulation game written in C for the **Game Boy Advance**.

> **Bunkered** is a hard science-fiction survival game focused on engineering, automation, resource management, and cooperation between survivors.

## About

World War III has begun.

A global nuclear exchange has made much of the Earth's surface uninhabitable. Underground, radiation-shielded bunkers have become humanity's last chance for survival.

But the bunker does not exist yet.

Before the war, you must select your initial survivors, use their money and skills to construct your shelter, purchase materials, and install the systems necessary to keep humanity alive.

When the war finally begins, the survivors enter the bunker.

From that point on, everything depends on how well you prepared.

## Gameplay

At the beginning of the game, you can create up to **three initial survivors**.

Each survivor has a class and a fixed specialization. Different survivors provide different advantages, and not every useful skill is necessarily useful at every stage of the game.

For example:

* An **investor** may contribute very little to the bunker after the war, but provide substantial financial resources during construction.
* A **botany professor** can improve the agricultural performance of nearby survivors.
* An **electrician** can be valuable for maintaining and repairing electrical infrastructure.
* A **hacker** can help exploit or defend computer networks.
* A **hunter** can contribute to obtaining resources outside the bunker.

The challenge is to build a group whose skills complement each other.

## Building the Bunker

Before the war, you must spend your available resources wisely.

Design and expand your bunker, purchase construction materials, and install the infrastructure required for long-term survival.

The bunker is built around interconnected systems such as:

* Electrical power generation and distribution
* Live and neutral wiring
* Water production and purification
* Ventilation
* Temperature control
* Lighting
* Automation
* Agriculture
* Life-support systems
* Electronics and control systems
* Resource storage

Power generation and consumption must be balanced.

Water must be treated.

Equipment can fail.

Systems can interact with each other in unexpected ways.

A bunker is not simply a collection of rooms — it is a complex system that must remain operational.

## Engineering and Hard Science Fiction

One of the main goals of Bunkered is to use **real scientific and engineering concepts as the foundation for its gameplay**.

The game does not attempt to be a perfect scientific simulator. Instead, real-world concepts are simplified and adapted into game mechanics.

Examples include:

### Electrical Engineering

Design electrical systems by routing power through the bunker and balancing generation against consumption.

### Automation

Build systems that automatically control temperature, lighting, ventilation, water, and other critical infrastructure.

### Electronics

Equipment can fail at the component level. Players may need to diagnose faults and replace electronic components on circuit boards.

### Water Treatment

Contaminated water can require filtration and chemical treatment before it becomes safe to use.

### Agriculture

Survivors with specialized knowledge can improve agricultural production and help other survivors become more effective.

### Cybersecurity

Rival bunkers may have vulnerable computer networks that can be infiltrated, while your own infrastructure must be protected against attacks.

The systems are designed to interact with one another, creating problems that require engineering and resource-management solutions rather than simply following predetermined objectives.

## The Outside World

The surface is dangerous.

Radiation, contamination, hostile creatures, damaged infrastructure, and limited resources make exploration a serious risk.

Survivors can only safely operate outside using appropriate protective equipment.

However, depending on how you manage your bunker, leaving it may eventually become necessary.

The wasteland may contain:

* Abandoned facilities
* Useful materials
* Food and water
* Equipment
* Other survivors
* Other bunkers
* Dangerous creatures
* Information about the world

Not every encounter has to be hostile.

Other bunkers can potentially become allies, trading partners, competitors, or enemies.

You may cooperate with them, trade resources, infiltrate their networks, hack their systems, or attack them.

## Development Philosophy

Bunkered is intended to be a **hard science-fiction game**, but the primary goal is still to make it fun.

Real science and engineering are used as inspiration for mechanics rather than as restrictions that make the game unnecessarily complicated.

The idea is to create situations where understanding how a system works can actually help the player solve a problem.

For example:

> Your bunker is consuming more electricity than it can generate.

The solution should not simply be "click an upgrade."

The player should be able to understand the problem and solve it through resource management, system design, automation, or engineering.

## Inspiration

One of my main inspirations was **The Martian**.

I particularly liked the idea of surviving with extremely limited resources and solving problems through science, engineering, improvisation, and experimentation.

The idea of growing potatoes using botanical knowledge was especially interesting to me.

I have also always been interested in radiation and nuclear disasters, which naturally led me toward bunkers, contaminated environments, mutated creatures, and post-nuclear survival.

The **Fallout** series also influenced some of the themes and ideas explored in the project, as did real-world nuclear disasters such as Chernobyl.

However, Bunkered is intended to be its own game.

My goal is to combine these influences with real scientific and technological concepts to create an original hard-science-fiction survival experience.

## Why the Game Boy Advance?

Bunkered originally started as a small experiment in the **Godot Engine** during a holiday.

I already had an interest in embedded systems and low-level programming, so I eventually decided to turn the project into a much more interesting technical challenge:

**What if I made it for the Game Boy Advance?**

The GBA is a fascinating platform for this kind of project.

Its hardware limitations force the game to be carefully designed around:

* Limited memory
* Limited processing power
* Small resolution
* Limited storage
* Simple input
* Retro graphics
* Hardware-specific programming

Instead of treating these limitations purely as obstacles, Bunkered is designed around them.

The project is therefore both a game and a personal exploration of **C programming, embedded systems, low-level development, and retro hardware**.

## Project Status

Bunkered is currently an **independent hobby project under development**.

The project is ambitious, but the intended scope is relatively focused.

I already have a clear idea of what I want to implement and, equally importantly, what I do not want to implement.

The main challenges are developing the skills and assets required to bring the project to completion, including:

* C programming
* Game Boy Advance development
* Pixel art
* Game design
* Retro music
* Sound design
* Game architecture
* Hardware limitations and optimization

I have created several other games throughout my life, including projects for the **Game Boy Color**, mostly as personal experiments.

Bunkered is another step in that process, but with the goal of eventually becoming a complete and playable game.

## Future Projects

I am also considering a potential second game built around a similar design philosophy, but set in a different survival scenario, such as deep-space survival or a zombie apocalypse.

Instead of a post-nuclear Earth, the player would have to survive in space while dealing with alien environments, limited resources, engineering problems, and potentially extraterrestrial life.

For now, however, Bunkered is the main project.
